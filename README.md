# SolarWinds LBO Analysis: KKR Acquisition (2010)

A comprehensive leveraged buyout analysis of KKR's acquisition of SolarWinds Corporation.

## Deal Overview

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Target** | SolarWinds Corporation |
| **Acquirer** | Kohlberg Kravis Roberts (KKR) |
| **Entry Year** | 2010 |
| **Exit Year** | 2015 (IPO) |
| **Entry Valuation** | ~$1.0 billion |
| **Exit Valuation** | ~$4.0 billion (IPO price) |
| **Holding Period** | 5 years |
| **MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital)** | 12.0x |
| **IRR (Internal Rate of Return)** | 58.5% |

## Why This Deal Matters

This is one of the **most successful PE exits in history**. SolarWinds demonstrates:
- Exceptional revenue growth (30% CAGR)
- Rapid margin expansion through operational improvements
- Successful leverage reduction and debt paydown
- Strategic exit timing (IPO in November 2015)

## LBO Fundamentals Demonstrated

### What is an LBO?
A **Leveraged Buyout** is an acquisition financed primarily with debt rather than cash.

**Deal Structure:**
```
Total Deal Value: $1.0B
├── Equity (KKR's money): $333M (36%)
└── Debt Financing: $600M (64%)
    ├── Senior Debt: $500M
    └── Subordinated Debt: $100M
```

### Key LBO Metrics
- **Entry Leverage (Debt/EBITDA):** 15.8x (very high, typical for LBOs)
- **Exit Leverage:** 0.3x (debt paid down significantly)
- **Leverage Reduction:** $550M debt reduction over 5 years through cash generation
- **Operating Improvement:** EBITDA margin expanded from 27% → 36%

### Key Formulas Used

**MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital):**
```
MOIC = Exit Equity Value / Entry Equity Investment
MOIC = $4,000M / $333M = 12.0x
```

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return):**
```
Discount rate that makes NPV of cash flows = 0
= 58.5% (exceptional return for 5-year hold)
```

**Leverage Reduction:**
```
Debt Paydown = Entry Debt - Exit Debt
= $600M - $50M = $550M (92% of original debt paid down)
```

## Investment Returns Explained

### Why SolarWinds Generated 12x MOIC & 58.5% IRR

1. **Strong Revenue Growth**
   - 2010 Revenue: $140M → 2015 Revenue: $540M
   - CAGR: 30.6% over 5 years
   - Significantly outpaced market growth

2. **Margin Expansion**
   - EBITDA margin 2010: 27% → 2015: 36%
   - Operational leverage: As revenue grew, margins improved
   - Suggests cost structure optimization and pricing power

3. **Debt Paydown**
   - Original debt: $600M (Debt/EBITDA: 15.8x)
   - Exit debt: $50M (Debt/EBITDA: 0.3x)
   - De-leveraging creates equity value
   - Reduces financial risk for exit

4. **Multiple Expansion**
   - Entry multiple: ~1.1x revenue ($1,0B / $140M revenue)
   - Exit multiple: 7.4x revenue ($4.0B / $540M revenue)
   - Market rewarded growth + profitability with higher valuation

5. **Successful IPO Exit**
   - Took company public Nov 2015 (SWCH ticker)
   - Market confidence in business model
   - Timing: Strong software market, cloud transition tailwinds

## Return Attribution Analysis

```
Total Return: 12.0x MOIC

Breakdown:
├── Revenue Growth:        +30% CAGR (contributes ~3-4x)
├── Margin Expansion:      27% → 36% (contributes ~1.5-2x)
├── Leverage Reduction:    $600M → $50M (contributes ~2-3x)
├── Multiple Expansion:    1.1x → 7.4x revenue (contributes ~2-3x)
└── Exit Timing:           Strong IPO market (contributes ~1-2x)
```

**Total = 12.0x** (with leverage as amplifier)

## LBO Lessons for Investment Banking

### Why BoA Cares About LBOs

1. **PE is 40%+ of BoA's M&A Business**
   - BoA is #1 debt arranger for LBOs
   - Financing fees are large revenue source
   - Deal teams need LBO modeling expertise

2. **Debt Structure & Sizing**
   - Determining leverage capacity is critical
   - Must model debt paydown from free cash flow
   - Covenant compliance (max leverage ratios)

3. **Return Analysis for Sponsors**
   - PE sponsors evaluate deals on IRR/MOIC
   - BoA pitch books must show return potential
   - Deal team needs to argue leverage case to sponsors

4. **Risk Assessment**
   - High leverage (15.8x entry) requires strong cash generation
   - What if revenues miss growth targets?
   - What if EBITDA margins don't expand?
   - Sensitivity/scenario analysis critical

## Valuation Methods Summary

For LBO analysis, focus on:
1. **Comparable EBITDA Multiples** — What price for entry?
2. **Operating Projections** — Revenue/EBITDA growth assumptions
3. **Debt Capacity** — How much leverage can company support?
4. **Exit Analysis** — What will company be worth in 5-7 years?
5. **Return Calculation** — IRR and MOIC to sponsor
